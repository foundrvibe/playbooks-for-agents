# Customer evidence

Use this to plan customer interviews, write interview questions, and turn interview notes into evidence. It follows the Y Combinator talk [How to talk to users](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/JQ-how-to-start-a-startup-talking-to-users), [The Mom Test](https://www.momtestbook.com/), and the jobs-to-be-done [switch interview](https://jobstobedone.org/switch-interview/).

## What counts as evidence

- **Past behavior beats opinion.** "Last month I lost two days to a missed approval" is evidence. "I would use that" is not.
- **Workarounds and spending.** What the person already does, builds, or pays to handle the problem. A spreadsheet they maintain is evidence of pain.
- **Commitments.** Time (a second meeting, a trial with their real work), reputation (an introduction to a colleague or their boss), or money (a deposit, a pre-order, a letter of intent). Compliments, "keep me posted", and "sounds great" are not commitments.
- **Strength.** Mark a signal `strong` when the person described a specific recent case or made a commitment. `weak` when they agreed in general. `none` when nothing came up.

## Interview questions

Ask about the person's life, not the idea. Do not pitch in the interview. Five core questions, adapted from the Y Combinator talk:

1. What is the hardest part about the task the idea touches?
2. Tell me about the last time that happened.
3. Why was that hard?
4. What, if anything, have you done to solve it?
5. What don't you love about the solutions you have tried?

Add one question on cost (time, money, or clients lost), one on who else is involved in the decision, and one ask for a commitment that fits the stage: a follow-up with their real data, an introduction, or a pre-order. Never ask "Would you use this?" or "How much would you pay?".

## Switching forces

Write the four forces from the [switch interview](https://jobstobedone.org/the-four-forces/). A customer switches when push and pull outweigh anxiety and habit.

- **Push:** what goes wrong with the current way.
- **Pull:** what the new way promises.
- **Anxiety:** what worries them about changing.
- **Habit:** what keeps them on the current way.

## Rules

- Never invent an interview, a person, a quote, or a result. Use only what the user supplied.
- Describe people by role and company type, not by name.
- Patterns usually repeat after five to eight interviews with one customer segment. Fewer than five is a starting point, not a conclusion.
- Keep conflicting evidence. When one interview contradicts another, record both and say what would settle it.
- Desk research shows where to find customers and how they describe the problem in public. It does not replace an interview.
