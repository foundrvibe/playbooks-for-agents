# Sales compliance

Use this before writing any outreach message or lead list. Research the rules for the playbook's `market_country`, and cite the regulator's own page. This is a summary for planning, not legal advice. Tell the user to confirm with a lawyer before a large campaign.

## Where to look

- **United States:** [CAN-SPAM Act compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) from the FTC. Commercial email needs accurate header information, a subject line that is not deceptive, identification as an ad, a valid postal address, and a clear way to opt out. Honor opt-outs within ten business days and keep the opt-out working for at least thirty days after sending. It applies to business-to-business email too.
- **Canada:** [CASL consent guidance](https://www.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email) and the [CRTC guide](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm). A commercial electronic message needs consent before it is sent: express consent, or implied consent such as an existing business relationship (generally up to two years after a purchase, or six months after an inquiry) or an address the person published without a statement refusing messages, where the message relates to their role. Identify the sender, include contact details, and process unsubscribes within ten business days. Keep proof of consent.
- **United Kingdom:** [ICO business-to-business marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing). Under PECR, email to corporate subscribers does not need prior consent, but sole traders and some partnerships count as individuals. UK GDPR still applies to named business contacts: identify yourself, offer an opt-out in every message, and keep a suppression list. Use a legitimate-interests assessment when relying on that basis.
- **European Union and other countries:** find the national data protection authority and its guidance on electronic marketing. Do not assume the US rules apply.

## Rules

- Use only lawful, public business sources: company websites, official registries, association directories, and listings the business published. Respect each site's terms.
- Do not buy or scrape personal email lists. Do not guess personal addresses.
- Record where each contact came from and, where consent is required, how it was given.
- Every message names the sender and gives a working way to opt out. Keep a do-not-contact list and check it before each send.
- Do not send messages for the user. Write them for the user to review and send.
