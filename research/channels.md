# Channels and positioning

Use this to position an offer, choose acquisition channels, and set launch metrics. Scope channels and events to the playbook's `target_region` when it is narrower than `market_country`. It follows April Dunford's [positioning components](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning), the [Bullseye framework](https://medium.com/@yegg/the-bullseye-framework-for-getting-traction-ef49d05bfd7e) from Traction, Paul Graham's [Do Things That Don't Scale](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html), and Y Combinator's [Setting KPIs and goals](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Iu-setting-kpis-and-goals).

## Positioning

Fill the components in this order. Each one rests on the one before.

1. **Competitive alternatives:** what buyers would use if this product did not exist, including spreadsheets, an agency, or doing nothing.
2. **Unique attributes:** what this product has that those alternatives lack.
3. **Value:** what those attributes let the customer do, in their words.
4. **Best-fit customers:** who cares most about that value, by observable traits.
5. **Market category:** the frame that makes the value obvious to that customer.

## Channels

- List candidate channels before choosing: founder network, direct outreach, communities, partnerships, content, search, marketplaces and directories, events, referrals, and paid ads.
- Pick about three to test in parallel. Each test is cheap and short, with a cost, a time box, and a success threshold set before it starts.
- After the tests, put most of the effort into the one channel that met its threshold. Drop the rest.
- Early customers usually come from manual work: the founder recruits them one by one, sets them up personally, and does by hand what the product will later automate.
- Open a page for every community, directory, or event you name. Do not name a channel you did not find.

## Metrics

- Pick one primary metric. Revenue is best once anyone pays; otherwise active customers using the core action.
- Set a weekly growth target for that metric. Paul Graham's [Startup = Growth](https://paulgraham.com/growth.html) calls five to seven percent a week a good rate during Y Combinator.
- Work the funnel backwards from the goal: customers needed, the conversion rate at each stage, and the contacts needed at the top. Label every rate you did not measure `Assumption:`.
