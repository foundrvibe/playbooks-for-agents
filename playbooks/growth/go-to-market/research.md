# Go-to-market plan

Read this after `schema.json`. Research the `market_country`, and write in the `document_language`. `template.md` is the result. These steps are the work.

A launch plan for the first customers is narrow and manual. Position against what buyers use today, test a few channels cheaply, then focus on the one that works. Follow [channels.md](../../../research/channels.md).

## Display

Keep each table cell to one or two sentences. Do not put a `|` inside a cell. Leave a blank line between sections. The `test_budget` slot draws the test costs; do not chart the other tables.

## Steps

1. Ask for `product`, `stage`, `target_customer`, and `market_country` when the context does not already answer them. Ask for `monthly_budget`, `team_capacity`, and `existing_audience`. Zero and "none yet" are answers. Wait.
2. Fill `competitive_alternatives` with what buyers would use if this product did not exist: the status quo, substitutes, and direct products. Use [competitors.md](../../../research/competitors.md) and open each website. On the web, list at least three.
3. Fill `positioning` in order: Competitive alternatives, Unique attributes, Value, Best-fit customers, Market category. Each rests on the one before. Write `value_proposition` as one sentence a best-fit customer would repeat.
4. Fill `buyer_roles` with the people who decide, pay, use, and influence. For a small business one person may play several parts; say so.
5. Fill `offer` with the launch offer and price hypotheses. Use the user's price first, then [pricing.md](../../../research/pricing.md). Label any price you chose `Assumption:`.
6. Fill `messaging` with one message per buyer role. `proof` is a customer result, an interview finding, or a cited page. When there is none, say what proof to collect.
7. Fill `channels` with candidates from [channels.md](../../../research/channels.md), starting with `existing_audience`. Open a page for each community, directory, event, or ad platform you name. On the web, list at least five. Mark about three `test now`, using the budget and team time; mark the rest `later` or `skip`.
8. Write one `channel_tests` row per `test now` channel. Set the cost, a time box of a few weeks, and the success threshold before the test starts. The total cost stays within `monthly_budget` unless the user agreed otherwise. Copy the channel names and costs into `test_budget` in the same order.
9. Set `primary_metric`: revenue when anyone pays, otherwise customers completing the core action. Set `weekly_target` with its basis.
10. Fill `launch_plan` with dated periods from preparation to the first review. The early periods include manual work: recruiting customers one by one and onboarding them personally. The last period ends with a review that keeps the channel that met its threshold and drops the rest.
11. Fill `kpis` with the funnel metrics that feed the primary metric, and `risks` with a response for each.
12. List every estimate in `assumptions`, and every page you opened in `sources`.

## Where to look

- `competitive_alternatives`: [competitors.md](../../../research/competitors.md).
- `offer`: the user's price, then [pricing.md](../../../research/pricing.md).
- `channels`: [channels.md](../../../research/channels.md). Trade associations and their events, communities, directories and marketplaces where the customer buys, and ad platforms. Judge each with [source-evaluation.md](../../../research/source-evaluation.md).
- Outreach in any channel follows [sales-compliance.md](../../../research/sales-compliance.md).

## Sources

- `sources`: one entry per page you opened, with title, publisher, URL, and the date you opened it. If you could not browse, set `research_mode` to `offline`, leave `sources` empty, and label every researched field `Assumption:`.
